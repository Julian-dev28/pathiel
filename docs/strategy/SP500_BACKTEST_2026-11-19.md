## Can 50x carry a stop

The rule on every desk: the stop may use at most half the distance to liquidation. From the venue's maintenance margin (1 / (2 x 50) = 1%):

| Leverage | Liquidation distance | Widest stop allowed | In median 1h ATRs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50x | 1.01% | 0.505% | 3.0 |
| 40x | 1.52% | 0.76% | 4.6 |
| 30x | 2.36% | 1.18% | 7.1 |
| 25x | 3.03% | 1.52% | 9.1 |
| 20x | 4.04% | 2.02% | 12.2 |

Yes, with a condition. A 0.505% stop is 3.0 hourly ATRs wide at the median and 1.6 at p90. It only works when the entry is at the structure: the shipped rule's stop sits 0.4% beyond the 24h edge, so the mid has to be within about 0.1% of that edge. In the backtest the median stop was 0.44% of price, every trade fit 50x, and of 73 stopped trades none filled more than 0.01% past its stop. 48 of 5,010 15m bars (1%) ranged more than 0.505%, and 4 ranged more than the whole 1.01% from entry to liquidation: about once every 13 days.

What 50x cannot do is trade oil's formula. Oil's stop floor is 0.8% of price, wider than the whole 0.505% budget: 0 entries in 208 days. At 20x with the 20/15 fit the oil formula took 8 trades in 208 days and lost 2.2R.

50x is not the problem and a lower leverage is not the fix. The same rule at a fixed lower leverage takes entries further from the edge and does worse:

| Leverage (fixed) | 1h trades | 1h net R | 1h tail | 1h stress | 15m net R |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50x | 103 | +10.64 | +7.69 | -14.09 | +3.57 |
| 40x | 109 | +0.06 | -3.54 | -24.16 | -4.81 |
| 30x | 90 | +5.31 | -2.14 | -14.37 | -6.62 |
| 25x | 90 | +5.32 | -2.14 | -14.33 | -6.62 |
| 20x | 90 | +5.32 | -2.14 | -14.33 | -6.62 |

No row holds up on the tail and under the stress. There is no "highest leverage that works" to name, because none of them has an edge to carry. 50x is at least not worse.

Dollar risk does not depend on the leverage. `place_bracket` sizes by `risk_profile.risk_per_trade_pct` of the desk's slice; 50x only sets how much margin the position posts and where liquidation sits.

## The rules tested

No S&P thesis has been journaled, so the side comes from the rule: `both` (long near the 24h low, short near the 24h high), `trend120` / `trend24` (only with the sign of that many hours' return).

- **edge24**: the oil desk's rule moved over. Market entry off the 24h range, stop beyond the 24h edge, target the far 24h edge, 2R, no entry after a break. Every entry is the live `next_trade.next_trade` call. Grid: stop buffer 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.3 / 2.0 x ATR14, floor 0 / 0.25% / 0.4% of price, target offset 0 / 0.1% / 0.5%, three side modes. 108 variants.
- **pull8**: trend filter plus pullback. Side from the 120h or 24h return, stop beyond the 8h edge, target the far 24h edge. Same grid without `both`. 72 variants.
- **orb**: the first 30 minutes of the US cash session, first 15m close outside it, stop at the far side or the middle, target 1.5 / 2 / 3 R, flat at 15:45 New York. 6 variants, 15m data only (37 sessions).

One position at a time. A decision at a bar's close, the path from the next bar. Inside a bar the stop is checked before the target, a bar that opens through the stop fills at its open, a target needs a trade through it.

Datasets: 1h (207 days, hourly decisions) and 15m (52 days, 15-minute decisions). Each is split 70/30 by time. A family's variant is picked on the first 70% of the 1h data (15 or more trades there), then its last 30% is read.

The bar, written in the script before the first run: EDGE needs 30 trades on 1h, net R above zero on the whole sample and the tail of both datasets, above zero under the cost stress, above zero without its two best trades, and half its grid neighbours above zero. LEANING: above zero on the whole 1h sample and its tail (5 or more tail trades), without its two best trades, with half its neighbours. Anything else is NOISE. One change after the first run: LEANING first asked only for a positive sample and tail, and called a 2-trade tail LEANING; the tail trade count and the two legs that catch a fluke (without the two best trades, the grid neighbours) were added then. It changed the opening-range row and nothing else.

## The rule that ships

Stop: the larger of 1.0 x ATR14 (1h) and 0.4% of the mid, beyond the 24h edge. Target: the far 24h edge, no offset. 2R. 50x only, never fitted lower. No entry after a break. In the test it takes both sides; live, the thesis picks the side and adds never-chase.

73 of the 103 trades stopped, 30 reached the target. Median stop 0.44% of price.

**Verdict on it: NOISE.** It fails three legs:

- Cost stress: -14.09R. The measured cost is 0.09R a trade and the whole edge is 0.10R a trade.
- Without its two best trades: -0.53R.
- Grid neighbours: 0.5 x ATR +14.84R, 1.3 x ATR -5.14R, floor 0.25% -11.57R, offset 0.1% -4.03R. One step in three directions flips the sign. Not a plateau.

And its tail is not held out. This cell was chosen after reading the whole sample (it is one of the two cells above zero on all three reads), so "+7.69R on the tail" is in-sample. The honest out-of-sample reads are the three family picks above, and they lose.

In account terms: the S&P slice is 5% of equity and a stop loses `risk_per_trade_pct` of the slice. At 6% a stop costs 0.3% of the account and the worst run here (14.4R) costs 4.3%. At 15% it is 0.75% and 10.8%. Compounded on the slice alone, the 1h sample ends at 1.00x with a 59% drawdown at 6% risk and at 0.13x with a 95% drawdown at 15%: a +10.6R total with a 29% win rate does not survive large bets.

Flattening loses 20R on 1h: it closes trades that were on their way to the target. Holding entries back is a wash. The desk has no weekend rule.

### Events

FOMC decisions in the sample (5): the release hour ranged 0.88% at the median, the four hours around it 1.55% (any four hours: 0.31%). The jobs report on first Fridays (7): 0.30% in the hour, 0.61% over four hours, past the 0.505% stop budget in 4 of 7 months (any four hours: 25%). The calendar generator has one CPI date inside the sample, so CPI has no reading here.

Holding entries back two hours either side of FOMC and CPI removed one trade from the 1h run (+11.72R against +10.64R) and none from the 15m run. The backtest cannot tell whether the blackout pays. It is on for the same reason as on oil: a release moves price further than the whole stop budget.

## What this does not test

- **The thesis.** Live, Claude writes a call and the rule only trades its side. Every desk's test here says the same thing: the rule alone is not the edge. Nobody has graded an S&P thesis yet. That is the missing test.
- **A regime.** 208 days, one market, +0.5% net with a 6.7% fall at the end.
- **Intrabar order.** On 1h bars a stop and a target inside one bar count as a stop. The 15m run is the finer read and is shorter.
- **Latency.** Entries fill at the bar's close plus 1 bp. The tick takes longer than that.
